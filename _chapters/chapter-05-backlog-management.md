# 5. Backlog Management

## 5.1 Purpose

The Product Backlog is the single authoritative source of work for the Scrum Team.

It contains all functional requirements, technical improvements, defects, and enablers required to evolve the product.

Backlog management ensures that development effort is aligned with business priorities and customer value.

---

## 5.2 Backlog Hierarchy

Philips adopts a hierarchical backlog structure inspired by SAFe.

```

Epic

↓

Capability

↓

Feature

↓

Story

↓

Task

```

### Epic

Represents a large business initiative.

Typical duration:

Several months.

---

### Capability

Represents a major solution capability delivered across one or more Agile teams.

---

### Feature

Represents a customer-visible functionality.

Typically delivered within one Program Increment.

---

### Story

Represents a small vertical slice of business value that can be completed within one Sprint.

---

### Task

Represents implementation work required to complete a Story.

Tasks are owned by Development Team members and are not tracked as business requirements.

---

## 5.3 Story Attributes

Every Story should include:

- Story Title
- Business Value
- Description
- Acceptance Criteria
- Story Point
- Owner
- Priority
- Dependencies

---

## 5.4 Definition of Ready (DoR)

A Story is considered Ready when:

- Business objective is clear.
- Acceptance Criteria are complete.
- Story size is appropriate.
- Dependencies are identified.
- Required documents are available.
- Team understands the requirement.

---

## 5.5 Definition of Done (DoD)

A Story is Done only when:

- Code is completed.
- Code Review passed.
- Unit Tests passed.
- Integration Tests passed.
- Acceptance Criteria verified.
- Documentation updated.
- Product Owner accepted.

---

## 5.6 Backlog Refinement

Backlog Refinement should occur continuously throughout the Sprint.

Objectives:

- Clarify future work.
- Reduce uncertainty.
- Improve estimation accuracy.
- Identify dependencies.

---

## 5.7 Prioritization

Prioritization should consider:

- Business Value
- Customer Impact
- Risk Reduction
- Technical Dependency
- Time Criticality

---

## 5.8 Common Anti-patterns

Avoid:

- Stories that are too large.
- Technical tasks without business value.
- Missing Acceptance Criteria.
- Undefined priorities.
- Excessive Work In Progress.
