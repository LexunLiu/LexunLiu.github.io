# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 Purpose

The purpose of this guide is to establish a unified Agile software development framework for Philips Shanghai R&D Center.

This guide provides practical guidance for managing software development projects by integrating industry best practices with Philips internal Product Development Lifecycle Management (PDLM) process.

It serves as the standard reference for all software development activities, ensuring consistent execution across different business units, Scrum Teams, and global collaboration environments.

This guide does **not** replace the official Scrum Guide or SAFe Framework. Instead, it provides organization-specific implementation guidance based on those frameworks and aligns them with Philips internal development processes.

---

## 1.2 Objectives

This guide aims to achieve the following objectives:

- Establish a common Agile development process across all software teams.
- Improve collaboration among cross-functional and distributed teams.
- Deliver customer value through iterative and incremental development.
- Improve transparency throughout the software development lifecycle.
- Build quality into every development activity.
- Reduce delivery risks through continuous inspection and adaptation.
- Align Scrum execution with Philips PDLM governance requirements.
- Standardize engineering practices and release management.
- Foster a culture of continuous learning and continuous improvement.

## 1.5 Agile Philosophy

Philips adopts Agile not merely as a development methodology, but as a mindset for delivering value continuously.

Our development philosophy is built upon the following principles:

### Customer Value First

Every development activity should contribute measurable value to customers.

---

### Transparency

Project status, risks, priorities, and progress should be visible to all stakeholders.

---

### Continuous Delivery

Software should be delivered in small, valuable increments instead of large, infrequent releases.

---

### Built-in Quality

Quality is everyone's responsibility and should be integrated into every stage of development rather than inspected at the end.

---

### Continuous Improvement

Every Sprint provides an opportunity to inspect, learn, and improve.

---

### Respect for People

High-performing teams are built on trust, collaboration, and shared ownership.

---

### Systems Thinking

Software development should be optimized as an end-to-end value stream rather than isolated functional activities.

## 1.8 Relationship Between Scrum, SAFe and PDLM

This guide integrates three complementary perspectives.

### Scrum

Provides team-level Agile practices including Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review, and Sprint Retrospective.

---

### SAFe

Provides organizational governance including Portfolio Management, Value Streams, Agile Release Trains (ART), Program Increment (PI), Lean Portfolio Management, and Flow Metrics.

---

### Philips PDLM

Provides mandatory governance activities, quality gates, compliance requirements, documentation, and engineering deliverables required throughout the product lifecycle.

The relationship can be summarized as follows:

| Framework | Primary Focus |
|------------|---------------|
| Scrum | Team Execution |
| SAFe | Enterprise Coordination |
| PDLM | Governance & Compliance |

This guide combines all three perspectives into one unified software development framework.

## 1.9 How to Use This Guide

The guide is organized into four parts.

### Part I — Agile Foundation

---

### Part II — Agile Execution

Describes how Scrum Teams execute work throughout each Sprint.

---

### Part III — Engineering Governance

Defines engineering practices, quality management, release management, metrics, and continuous improvement.

---

### Part IV — Philips PDLM Activities

Contains the complete Philips PDLM Activity Checklist.

**Important**

The PDLM Activities Checklist is the official operational reference used within Philips Shanghai R&D Center.

This guide supplements the checklist by explaining:

- Why each activity exists.
- When it should be performed.
- Who is responsible.
- Expected inputs and outputs.
- Recommended best practices.

The original checklist remains unchanged and serves as the authoritative operational reference.
