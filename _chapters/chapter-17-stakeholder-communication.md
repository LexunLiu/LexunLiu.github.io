# 17. Stakeholder Communication

## 17.1 Purpose

Effective communication enables alignment among stakeholders, supports informed decision-making, and increases transparency throughout the software development lifecycle.

Communication should be timely, accurate, concise, and audience-oriented.

---

# 17.2 Stakeholder Categories

Typical stakeholders include:

- Customers
- Product Management
- Engineering Management
- Development Teams
- QA Teams
- Operations
- Vendors
- Regulatory Bodies

---

# 17.3 Communication Principles

Communication should be:

- Transparent
- Consistent
- Action-oriented
- Evidence-based
- Respectful

---

# 17.4 Communication Channels

Examples include:

- Sprint Review
- Daily Scrum
- PI Planning
- Steering Committee
- Weekly Status Report
- Release Announcement
- Incident Notification

---

# 17.5 Status Reporting

Project status reports should include:

- Overall Status
- Sprint Progress
- Risks
- Issues
- Dependencies
- Decisions
- Milestones

Status reports should focus on actionable information rather than excessive detail.

---

# 17.6 Decision Log

Significant project decisions should be documented.

Typical fields include:

- Decision ID
- Description
- Decision Maker
- Date
- Rationale
- Impact

---

# 17.7 RAID Management

Projects should maintain a RAID Log.

RAID includes:

- Risks
- Assumptions
- Issues
- Dependencies

RAID should be reviewed regularly by the project leadership team.

---

# 17.8 Escalation

Escalation should occur when:

- Critical risks emerge.
- Delivery commitments are threatened.
- Cross-team dependencies cannot be resolved.
- Customer impact becomes significant.
