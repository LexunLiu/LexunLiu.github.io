# 7. Agile Planning & Estimation

## 7.1 Overview

Planning provides alignment while estimation provides predictability.

The objective of Agile planning is not to predict the future with certainty, but to create sufficient transparency for informed decision-making.

Planning occurs at multiple levels:

- Portfolio Planning
- PI Planning
- Release Planning
- Sprint Planning
- Daily Planning

## 7.3 Estimation Principles

Agile estimation should be:

- Relative
- Collaborative
- Experience-based
- Continuously refined

Story Points measure complexity rather than effort.

Story Points should never be converted directly into working hours.

---

## 7.4 Story Point Estimation

Typical Story Point sequence:

- 1
- 2
- 3
- 5
- 8
- 13
- 20
- 40
- 100

Story Point estimation considers:

- Complexity
- Risk
- Uncertainty
- Effort

---

## 7.5 Planning Poker

Planning Poker is the preferred estimation technique.

Process:

1. Explain Story
2. Clarify Questions
3. Individual Estimation
4. Reveal Cards
5. Discuss Differences
6. Reach Consensus

---

## 7.6 Capacity Planning

Capacity Planning determines available team effort for a Sprint.

Factors include:

- Team Size
- Vacation
- Public Holidays
- Support Activities
- Technical Debt
- Planned Training

---

## 7.7 Velocity

Velocity represents the average number of Story Points completed during previous Sprints.

Velocity should be used for forecasting only.

Velocity should never be used as a performance KPI.

---

## 7.8 Release Forecasting

Release forecasts should consider:

- Team Velocity
- Feature Size
- Team Capacity
- Dependencies
- Risks

Forecasts should be reviewed after every Sprint.

---

## 7.9 WSJF Prioritization

Philips recommends using Weighted Shortest Job First (WSJF) when prioritizing Features at the Program level.

WSJF considers:

- Business Value
- Time Criticality
- Risk Reduction
- Job Size

Higher WSJF indicates higher implementation priority.

---

## 7.10 Common Anti-patterns

Avoid:

- Estimating in hours
- Comparing team velocities
- Assigning Story Points by managers
- Treating estimates as commitments
- Ignoring uncertainty
